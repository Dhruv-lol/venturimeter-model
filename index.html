Model assumptions

Steady, incompressible flow through a horizontal pipe; uniform velocity at each section; no elevation change. The Report CFD case option displays the report’s two simulated cases. Live ideal Bernoulli ignores head loss in the pressure equation. Bernoulli with estimated losses subtracts inlet-pipe, converger, half-throat friction, and contraction loss when estimating throat pressure.

h_f = Σ f(L/D)V²/(2g)   ·   h_m = Σ K V²/(2g)

Reading the pressure heads

Pressure heads use gauge pressure: h = p_gauge/(ρg), shown as metres of the selected fluid. The outlet boundary is fixed at atmospheric pressure: p_out = 0 kPa gauge. Inlet gauge pressure remains adjustable. Differential head is Δh = (p₁−p₂)/(ρg); the common atmospheric reference cancels from this difference.

The report’s throat values are reproduced as reported CFD gauge pressures. They fall below −101.3 kPa gauge, implying negative absolute pressure at standard atmosphere; treat them as solver/reference outputs that need validation, not physically realizable liquid pressures. The head-loss values are estimates: commercial steel roughness is set to 0.045 mm; friction uses Darcy–Weisbach with Colebrook friction factors. Minor losses include only the conical contraction and diffuser. No entry, exit, bends, or valves are included.

The report geometry is based on 40 cm inlet and 20 cm throat diameters (areas 1,256.6 cm² and 314.2 cm²). The diameters imply areas of about 1,256.6 cm² and 314.2 cm², consistent with your correction. The report’s separate 800 cm² / 400 cm² area figures conflict with both the stated diameters and the stated 0.25 area ratio; the model uses the diameter-derived areas. CFD benchmark pressures and reported peak throat speeds are fixed to the report cases; peak speeds are local contour maxima, while the loss estimate uses section-average continuity velocity. Major loss integrates Darcy friction across report-based lengths and the variable-diameter transitions. Minor losses use the editable Kc and Kd values. Changing geometry or operating controls switches to a live calculation. Ideal flow rate is Q = A₁V₁. Water and diesel viscosity use the report values (1.003 mPa·s and 2.4 mPa·s); diesel is treated as constant-viscosity for this estimate, so shear-thinning behavior is not modeled. Diesel density varies by grade and temperature; custom fluid inputs include viscosity.
